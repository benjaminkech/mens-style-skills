# Style-book design: illustrated style guide

The book is a **practical, illustrated style guide about him**, like the how-to guides of a good style coach or style app. It is not a fashion magazine and not a report. Each page teaches a few rules and shows them with pictures, so he can apply them the next morning. Build pages as a visual design/page artifact if the environment offers one, otherwise as self-contained HTML. Write in the user's language, in a plain, direct second person.

Use this format and these patterns, but don't copy any brand's name, logo, illustrations or text.

## The core pattern: rule → why → picture → ✓/✗

Every chapter is a short sequence of rule blocks:

1. **Rule headline:** a short instruction in bold sans-serif, e.g. "Navy statt Schwarz am Gesicht", "Halslinie zwei Finger über dem Adamsapfel", "Maximal drei sichtbare Lagen".
2. **Why:** one or two sentences, e.g. "Schwarz ist zu hart für deinen niedrigen Kontrast und vertieft Schatten unter den Augen."
3. **Picture:** one of the visual types below, built from his photo or his own clothes.
4. **✓ / ✗ captions** under the picture: a green check for what works, a red cross for what doesn't, each with a one-line reason. Use them wherever a contrast teaches something.

Show more and say less. If a block needs more than three sentences, split it into two rules.

## Page anatomy

- **Chapter label** at the top in the accent color, small (e.g. "Dein Style Guide › Farben").
- **Hero image:** a wide picture under the label, either a crop of his photo or a row of his items.
- **Title:** "<Topic>", optionally with "101" or "für dich", large bold sans-serif.
- **Intro:** one sentence, then 3–4 bullets "Kurz gesagt".
- **2–4 rule blocks** (the core pattern).
- **"Deine Regeln" box** at the end: a tinted box with 3–5 checkbox lines summarizing the page, so he can screenshot it.
- **Footer:** page number, plus his first name and the date.

## Visual types

| Type | Use for | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Product row with arrows** | Sequences: layer order, building an outfit | His items as cutouts on a white panel, left to right, small arrows or "+" between them, a label under each |
| **Do / Don't pair** | Color near the face, fit, combinations | Two images or swatch blocks side by side, ✓ caption under the good one, ✗ under the bad one |
| **Drape row** | His colors on his face | 3 drape images of his photo side by side (same crop, same size), color name under each, then one ✓ or ✗ line per image on skin, eyes and contours. One row for best colors, one for colors to avoid, so the difference shows at a glance |
| **Annotated photo** | Beard lines, glasses, collar | His photo crop with thin lines and short labels |
| **Zone map** | Where grooming happens | His portrait with soft, blurred color zones (head, brows, eye area, cheek and neck lines, upper lip) and a small legend; numbered routine steps beside it |
| **Profile meters** | Contrast, depth, brightness | A label, the value in words and a thin bar filled to low / medium / high; one row each, beside his portrait and season |
| **Option cards** | Beard styles, frame shapes | Schematic drawings from `assets/schematics.md` in a row, rating label above the name (top pick / very good / good / avoid), length or color and one line below |
| **Length scale** | Beard length | 0 / 3 / 6 / 10 / 15+ mm as five cells, his recommended range filled in the accent color, one line under each |
| **Swatch row** | Palettes | Color fields with name and hex below; group them as best / good / avoid |
| **Outfit formula** | Outfits from his wardrobe | 3–4 product images in a row with "+" between, then one line: occasion and why it works |
| **Mini table** | Rhythms and routines (trim every 2–3 days, SPF daily) | Two columns with hairlines, no borders |

## Look

- Portrait pages, 1080 × 1350 px (or A4), with a single main column (max ~760 px of text width) and wide margins. Visual panels may use the full content width.
- Background warm off-white `#F7F3EC`; text `#1F1F1F`; muted `#6B675F`; accent dark green `#1F3A2E`; tinted box `#EFE3D3`; visual panels white `#FFFFFF`; ✓ green `#2E7D4F`; ✗ red `#B23A2E`.
- **Headings:** a friendly geometric sans-serif in bold (e.g. Manrope, DM Sans). Title 52–60 px, rule headlines 26–30 px.
- **Body:** Inter or similar, 15–16 px, line height 1.55.
- Clean and calm: no gradients, no decorative frames, no emoji (✓ and ✗ are characters, not emoji). Rounded corners at most 6 px on panels.
- Contrast at least 4.5:1, and labels never on top of a swatch.

## Images

- **His photo** (only with his consent, after the photo check): crop with `scripts/prepare_images.py` into a portrait (cover), a zone map base (page 4), a beard close-up (page 5) and an eye-line crop (page 6).
- **Drape images** (page 2), colors and metals: made from the same photo with `mens-color-analysis/scripts/make_drapes.py`. Show them all at the same crop and size, so only the fabric color changes between them; a drape that covers the beard or chin is re-made, not used.
- **His clothes:** clean product cutouts on white, `object-fit: contain`. Skip images showing another person's face or with baked-in text. For ✗ examples, use his own off-palette items (e.g. the black turtleneck), so the lesson is about his actual wardrobe.
- Never use stock photos, illustrations or AI images to stand in for him or his clothes. Schematic option drawings (`assets/schematics.md`) are fine because they compare styles, not show him; label them as schematic.
- Photos of him and shop product photos stay in his private document. They never go into public repos or shared templates.

## Don't

- Magazine theatrics: full-bleed cover portraits, giant display type, poetic headlines.
- Report style: tables of findings as the main content, rows of bordered cards.
- Rules without a picture or a ✓/✗ example.
- More than ~120 words of running text per page.
