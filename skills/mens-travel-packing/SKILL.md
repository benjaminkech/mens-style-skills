# Men's Travel Packing

Build one packing list he can tick off, made from **his own clothes**, that covers every day of the trip with as few pieces as possible. Answer in the user's language. The user may be packing for someone else: address that person.

If `STYLE-PROFILE.md` exists, read it first: wardrobe, palette, body, dress code. If he has a wardrobe list elsewhere (spreadsheet, app export), read that. Use only items he owns. Suggest buying something only if he asks, or if the trip truly needs an item he lacks (e.g. a warm coat for −15 °C, a plug adapter). Then name it in one line, separately.

## 1. Trip facts (ask only what's missing, at most 4 questions in one go)

Destination and exact city (climate differs a lot within a country), dates or number of nights, purpose and dress code (meetings, dinners, a formal event, beach), laundry (hotel laundry, every few days, none), luggage (carry-on only or checked). Departure city and weather on the travel day matter too.

**Look up what changes:** the forecast or typical climate for those dates (temperature range, humidity, rain, sun) and anything current that affects packing: entry rules for his passport and the trip's purpose, plug type, local dress norms for temples, offices or religious sites. State where each fact came from. Entry rules depend on purpose: a visa exemption "for tourism" may not cover a work trip, so flag that clearly and point him to the official source; he isn't getting legal advice from you.

## 2. Build the capsule

- **One color base.** Two or three neutrals from his palette (e.g. navy, white, grey) plus at most one accent, so every top works with every bottom. This is what lets fewer pieces make more outfits.
- **Count by laundry cycle, not by days.** Tops ≈ days until laundry + 1; bottoms 2–3 for up to two weeks; shoes 2 pairs (+1 only for a formal event or sport). With laundry every 4–5 days, pack for 5 days, however long the trip.
- **Weather first.** Heat and humidity: breathable cotton, linen, light merino, polos, loose fit, nothing that shows sweat (avoid light grey and silk for long days). Strong AC indoors: one light layer. Rain: a compact layer or umbrella, shoes that dry. Cold: warm layers that stack (merino → knit → coat), wear the bulkiest pieces on the plane.
- **Dress code.** Cover the most formal moment of the trip with one outfit, then make everything else slightly less formal. Smart casual travels best: one versatile jacket or overshirt lifts any outfit.
- **Travel-day outfit.** Comfortable, layered for both climates (departure and arrival), bulky items worn rather than packed, slip-on shoes for security. Leave a heavy winter coat at home if he can get to the airport without it.
- **Proportions and palette** from his profile still apply (see `mens-style-advisor` and `mens-color-analysis`).

## 3. Output

Short and skimmable, as a checklist he can tick off:

1. **Trip in one line:** city, dates or nights, weather range, dress code, laundry, luggage.
2. **Heads-up:** only what needs action before he leaves (entry rules, adapter, medication, a missing item). Skip if nothing.
3. **Wear on the plane:** one outfit.
4. **Clothes:** checkboxes by category with his exact items (color + item), counts in brackets.
5. **Outfits:** 4–6 formulas from the packed items, one line each: `Top + Layer + Bottom + Shoes`, labeled by day type (office, client dinner, weekend, travel).
6. **Grooming & health:** what his routine needs at the destination (e.g. trimmer and charger, SPF for a shaved head in strong sun, insect repellent).
7. **Tech & documents:** passport, entry documents, cards, adapter, chargers, insurance details.
8. **Leave at home:** 2–4 tempting items that won't earn their space, with the reason.

Keep the whole list on one screen where possible. Offer to keep it as a checklist he can share (a sheet or a doc), and offer a printable one-pager in the design of `mens-style-book/references/design.md` if he wants one.
