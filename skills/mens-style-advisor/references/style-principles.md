# Style principles

Opinions this collection holds. They are guidelines, not hard rules: apply them with judgment, and when breaking one looks better, say why.

- **Fit before everything.** Shoulders must fit; hems, waists and sleeves are cheap to tailor. Tailoring beats buying.
- **Few colors, one focal point.** Usually two or three colors per outfit (neutral whites and small accessories don't count), and at most one pattern *or* one bold color. Calm outfits look deliberate.
- **Light with light, dark with dark** for the main pieces, with one intentional contrast. Low-contrast men look best tonal.
- **Texture over pattern and logos.** Flannel, oxford cloth, suede, knit and tweed add interest without risk.
- **Layers step up smoothly.** Weight and formality increase from inside to outside without big jumps (a shirt or fine knit bridges a T-shirt and an overcoat). About three visible layers stay readable.
- **Lighter colors near the skin, darker outside**, except in summer or a deliberate tonal look.
- **Collars and knitwear by formality.** A V-neck frames a collar and tie for business; a crewneck over a collared shirt works for smart casual when the collar sits neatly.
- **Formality stays within one step** across an outfit (no athletic sneakers with a worsted suit).
- **Colors near the face follow his color analysis**; elsewhere there is more freedom.
- **Proportions:** shorter men and short legs → higher rise, no trouser break, tonal lower half, shorter jackets and coats; slim frames gain from layers; an open darker outer layer slims larger frames.
